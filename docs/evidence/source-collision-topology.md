# Source collision walk, mounted boundaries and physical route ownership

The eight controlled native families contain **168,822 matching observations**.
The controlled collision/topology producers and public graph regressions cover
mixed callback order, dense actors, mounted jumps and boundary response, physical
fork choice, unequal merges, independent bike/body paths, route-specific finish
ownership, offbranch traffic contacts and source scenery beyond the initial Dual
cache. This is bounded stage-2 evidence. Combat/police, complete changing-terrain
recovery, full career walkthrough and replacement release acceptance remain
separate completion stages. Aggregate receipts are in
[source-collision-topology.json](source-collision-topology.json).

## Callback transactions and incidents

New source factories explicitly select `contact_walk_version: 1`. Each sorted
native-style outer object meets its statics before later object pairs, with the
source longitudinal early exit. Callback-updated poses and captured impact
velocities feed subsequent callbacks. The controlled producer exercises 3, 4, 8
and 16 riders, heights, statics, independently detached bodies and a fresh body
activated during the same walk. All 576 transactions match the actual pinned C++
producer. Its frozen version-0 baseline has 197 mismatches. A separate valid
intermediate baseline exposed eight missing fresh-body callbacks; the original
480-case version had not placed the third actor at the body's height.

Static incident identity now includes the owning bike/body identity. Actor 3's
bike and actor 1's detached body previously aliased through the old untyped key.
Adjacent/overlapping panels on the same physical facade share an incident;
separated or different facades retain independent incidents. Public regressions
cover sustained contact, separation/reimpact, simultaneous facades and cache
reconstruction after serialization. These are Rust incident/event-policy tests;
no claim is made that the native engine emits Rust incident events.

The mounted terrain producer follows actual `Race.step_rider` and
`collide_riders` through 67,142 ticks in 2,160 episodes, stopping at the first
crash. A diagnostic copy calls the pinned think, traction, integration, move,
wall and vertical routines separately and asserts agreement with the actual
whole step before exposing the wall-response flag. It covers building 0,
mountain/cliff 1, tunnel 3, hill 4 and open 255, short/tall rails, left/right,
zero/500 road offsets, three speeds, ground/airborne starts, changing grade,
adjacent panels/openings and held steering away then back. The legacy
surface/motion baseline has 54,422 mismatches against the identical trace; the
new phased mounted transaction has zero.

The native trace contains 18,756 observable boundary responses, 722 episodes
with consecutive responses, 698 crossing sample boundaries while scraping,
468 episodes with separation then reimpact and 60 with scripted held-away/back
reimpact. None of these mounted boundary ticks reduces stamina or bike condition;
506 episodes end in a first crash. The result preserves that source behavior
rather than adding damage every tick. Detached recovery after that crash is not
part of this trajectory comparison. The parent's separate [terrain query, height and real-data geometry
inventory](source-terrain-boundaries.md) cover the imported surface kernel and
selectors.

## Motion, height and crossing scope

`motion_version: 3` retains the source grounded soft-return skid marker and
initial bike contact word 37 (object behavior word 11), rather than the global
74 contact base. Mounted source motion is phased so wall response precedes
vertical integration and uses the post-wall ground query. Earlier profile
versions retain their historical order and defaults.

The expanded mounted contact producer contains 90,450 exact native/Rust tick
observations in 1,005 trajectories across all 15 bike specifications. Flat,
crest/drop, solid/short/overhead geometry, steering and source endpoint queries
are exercised. Of those observations, 14,850 start with a real native
3,000-tick throttle-from-rest warmup, preserving the attained speed, gear and
handling state and translating only longitudinal placement onto the contact
fixture. The 30,000 and 1,000,000 initial-speed groups are explicitly injected
states, not claims of ordinary reachability. One million is the supported seed
speed ceiling. Its maximum observed forward step is 3,902 Q8; the 30,000 group
has a 117-Q8 maximum.

An independent actual native reachability measurement starts at rest for each
of the 15 specifications, holds throttle with/without nitro for 12,000 ticks and
retains the source charge limits. Its 30 flat-road trajectories reach a maximum
speed of 21,968 and an 85-Q8 forward step, with 14,411 nitro-active ticks. This
measurement is not an additional differential observation count or a proof of a
universal speed bound on every authored course.

The source contact detector remains endpoint-only. Injected extreme-speed
trajectories can cross an entire narrow obstacle without response; the tests
preserve that source result instead of silently introducing continuous collision
detection. The source-warmed group has no whole-narrow-obstacle crossings.
Exact rational segment/rectangle tests distinguish real diagonal
misses from overlap of the swept axis-aligned bounding box. All 60 diagonal fixtures match their obstacle-free native controls for
5,400 ticks. Of those, 34 prove a swept-box false positive, including four
source-warmed reachable trajectories; the remaining warmed fixtures are ordinary
disjoint misses. The old injected 259/260/261-Q8 cases remain endpoint-policy evidence,
not ordinary maximum-speed reachability evidence.

## Steering, offsets and physical ownership

Source setups carry optional versioned `Roads` metadata with bounded physical
nodes, all branch-mask profiles, raw ground and per-profile source sample/cache
identities. The 26 profiles across the five courses use the branch mask itself
as the serialized `Unit.source_route`/`Body.source_route` value. The importer
retains nonzero road offsets and physical scenery for every node. Derived
per-actor configurations/topologies and facade/cache lookup structures are not
serialized and rebuild deterministically. Validation rejects inconsistent mask
ordering, ground/identity budgets, invalid origins and route references.

Actual pinned `move_along` crossings compare 198 edge-minus-one/equal/plus-one
Q8 cases across every course, mask and fork. The frozen preselected-route
baseline has 132 mismatches. Source choice uses the raw lateral pose at actual
forward boundary crossing: native longitudinal movement precedes that tick's
lateral update. Another 9,504 native ticks in 1,782 controlled held-steering
trajectories vary entry position and 5,000/15,000/30,000 initial speeds to prove
that route selection is not committed a sample early. Here too the initial
speed is a controlled fixture input, not a from-rest reachability assertion.

Contact callbacks can also displace a rider through a fork. The actual native
callback producer exposes 132 wrong-path/half results among 198 cases when the
old loaded portal overrides the callback pose. `advance_contact` now selects the
physical fork from that pose; all physical words and path/half outputs match.
Active BORR half history and route configuration follow the updated owner.

Bike and detached-body routes remain independent. Public multi-tick and replay
regressions place linked objects on different fork arms, verify their ground and
scenery contexts and follow them through unequal merges. Per-profile source
cache queries beyond the original Dual cache match 624 actual native scenery
transactions at branch offsets, with classes 0, 3 and 16 and height edges. A
130-case pinned-native finish-event inventory verifies all 26 masks and five
levels. Before/at/after finish tests use the rider's current route length;
standings expose distance remaining through optional `Unit.race_progress`.

Physical same-road/adjacent-road collision bridges rebase the other object's
current and captured previous positions into each callback owner's axis, then
restore pushed objects to their own accumulated-distance axes. This also fixes
a reproduced offbranch traffic panic from unwrapping an initial-route projection.
Public cases cover an actual vehicle contact off the initial route, shared-tail
vehicle response despite unequal accumulated branch lengths, a dense four-rider
shared-merge transaction invariant under different elapsed axes, disconnected roads
and independent bike/body traffic targets. Ordinary traffic movement policy is
retained; these tests do not claim full native traffic AI trajectories.

The pinned `TrackRing` split/merge walkers are explicitly unsupported. Whole
mixed collision walks use a supported straight ring; fork comparisons call the
actual supported movement/callback functions. Rust shared-merge ordering,
loading and rebasing are graph functional adaptations, with source primitive
responses, not claimed whole native Ring/full Race split/merge parity. The
existing isolated seed-1 scenery placement and fixed-60-Hz decision policies are
unchanged. The parent owns route-aware rendering/camera comparisons, native-art
branch episodes and final all-factory/replay integration checks; see
[physical-road-presentation.md](physical-road-presentation.md).
